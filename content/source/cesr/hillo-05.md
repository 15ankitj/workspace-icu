# 🚨 HiLLO 5 — Resuscitation, stabilisation and transfer

## What the curriculum asks for
*"Doctors specialising in Intensive Care Medicine can identify, resuscitate and stabilise a critically ill patient, as well as undertake their safe intra-hospital or inter-hospital transfer to an appropriately staffed and equipped facility."*

## At a glance
- **Standard expected:** Level 4
- **Key capabilities:** 11, across three subpages: Recognition and initial management (5.1–5.4), Airway, ventilation and transfer (5.5–5.6), Communication, escalation and safety (5.7–5.11)
- **Anchor evidence:** General Adult ICM logbook reflecting a **minimum 2¼ years' experience**, transfer evidence, valid ALS/ATLS
- See: **Evidence rules that apply everywhere**

# KCs 5.1–5.4 — Recognition and initial management

## KC 5.1 — Identify an acutely ill patient or one at risk of significant deterioration by taking account of their medical history, clinical examination, vital signs and available investigations
- [ ] ACAT: unselected referrals/ward reviews identifying deteriorating patients
- [ ] Mini-CEX: structured assessment of an acutely ill patient
- [ ] Logbook: deteriorating-patient reviews and outcomes
**My evidence for 5.1** —

## KC 5.2 — Integrate clinical findings with timely and appropriate investigations to form a differential diagnosis and an initial treatment plan
- [ ] CBD: undifferentiated critical illness worked to a plan
- [ ] Mini-CEX: admission clerking with differential and plan
**My evidence for 5.2** —

## KC 5.3 — Administer intravenous fluids and inotropic drugs as clinically indicated utilising central venous access where required and monitoring the effectiveness of these treatments with invasive monitoring techniques
- [ ] DOPS: central venous access; arterial access
- [ ] CBD: shock managed with fluids/vasoactive drugs guided by invasive monitoring
- [ ] Logbook: vascular access and haemodynamic support episodes
**My evidence for 5.3** —

## KC 5.4 — Stabilise and initiate an initial treatment plan for a critically ill acute surgical, acute medical or peri-partum patient including those with sepsis or post-trauma and institute timely antimicrobial therapy
- [ ] CBD: sepsis with timely antimicrobials; CBD: trauma or peri-partum stabilisation
- [ ] ACAT: acute surgical/medical take contribution
- [ ] Logbook: breadth across surgical, medical, obstetric, trauma, sepsis presentations

# KCs 5.7–5.11 — Communication, escalation and safety

## KC 5.7 — Communicate effectively and in a timely manner, with fellow members of the multi-disciplinary team including those from other specialties and make an accurate, legible and contemporaneous entry in the patient's medical record
- [ ] Mini-CEX: MDT communication during an emergency
- [ ] MSF comments on communication; anonymised documentation example
**My evidence for 5.7** —

## KC 5.8 — Where escalation of care is required, be able to arrange this and provide a succinct structured handover to clinical colleagues
- [ ] ACAT or Mini-CEX: structured handover (e.g. SBAR) at escalation
- [ ] Referral letter/email arranging escalation (anonymised)
**My evidence for 5.8** —

## KC 5.9 — Recognise when a patient has the potential to deteriorate or requires future treatment escalation and be able to provide explicit instructions regarding an ongoing treatment plan and contact details should a further review be required
- [ ] CBD: ward review with explicit escalation plan and safety-netting
- [ ] Anonymised plan documentation demonstrating explicit instructions
**My evidence for 5.9** —

## KC 5.10 — Have the ability to communicate with a patient's family, in terms they can understand, the patient's clinical condition, current and likely future treatment options and where possible, an indicative prognosis in an empathetic and understanding manner
- [ ] Mini-CEX: family discussion including prognosis
- [ ] Reflection on a difficult family conversation; patient/family feedback if available
**My evidence for 5.10** —

## KC 5.11 — Be mindful at all times that whilst assessing and treating patients they must maintain optimum safety for their patients by recognising any limitations of their current clinical environment, the available equipment and personnel and employing best practice guidelines where these exist
- [ ] CBD or reflection: adapting care to environment/equipment/staffing limits (e.g. resus in ED, night staffing)
- [ ] Evidence of guideline use in emergency care; incident report with learning if relevant
**My evidence for 5.11** —
